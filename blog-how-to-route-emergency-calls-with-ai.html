Why Binary Emergency Routing Fails (And What to Do Instead)

Binary emergency routing — where calls are either "emergencies" or "not emergencies" — consistently produces one of two failure modes: you get called for everything, or you get called for nothing. Neither is sustainable. The fix is a three-tier system that matches response speed to actual urgency without burning out the business owner.

Most AI receptionist setups give you a simple toggle: "transfer this call immediately if the caller uses these keywords." Business owners turn it on, add a few words like "emergency" and "urgent," and move on. Within a week, they're getting transferred into calls where someone just wanted to see if they're open on Saturdays. Within a month, they stop picking up the transfers because the signal-to-noise ratio is too low. Then a real emergency gets missed.

The 3-Tier Call Classification Framework replaces the binary switch with three routing behaviors:

  • Tier 1 — Emergency (Transfer Now): The AI immediately alerts you via SMS and calls your cell phone. The caller stays on the line while the transfer connects. This is for situations where a human needs to be on the phone within 60 seconds — not because it's good customer service, but because the physical or financial damage is actively worsening.
  • Tier 2 — Urgent (Priority SMS, Call Back Within 30 Minutes): The AI captures full details, sends you an urgent SMS, and sets the expectation with the caller that you'll call back shortly. This tier handles situations that are time-sensitive but not actively deteriorating — a first-time patient with acute pain, a commercial client whose system is down during business hours, a prospect who's comparing two quotes and needs an answer today.
  • Tier 3 — Routine (Book the Appointment): The AI handles it completely. Books the appointment, sends you the summary, and the caller gets a confirmed time slot. You see it when you check your dashboard.

The critical insight: Tier 2 is what most setups skip entirely. Without it, you're forced to choose between routing everything to Tier 1 (constant interruptions) or routing too much to Tier 3 (missed urgency). Tier 2 captures the middle 40-60% of calls that need a fast human response but don't require you to stop what you're doing this instant.

Step 1: Define Your Tier 1 Keywords by Industry

Emergency call detection works by matching what the caller says against a keyword list you configure. "Emergency" and "urgent" are the default keywords in most systems — and they're the wrong ones to rely on, because callers don't say "I have an emergency." They describe what's happening. The keyword list below gives you a starting point by industry, built around how callers actually talk, not how we categorize their problems.

Copy the list that matches your business and paste it directly into your CallBird AI configuration. Remove anything that doesn't apply to your service area or trade.

HVAC & Plumbing

  • No heat / heat went out / furnace not working / no hot water
  • No air / no AC / air conditioning stopped
  • Water everywhere / flooding / burst pipe / pipe burst / water leaking through ceiling
  • Gas smell / gas leak / smell gas
  • Frozen pipes / pipes froze
  • Sewer backing up / sewage backup / toilet overflowing into floor

Electrical

  • No power / power out / breaker won't reset
  • Sparking / sparks / burning smell / smoke from outlet
  • Electric shock / got shocked
  • Panel on fire / burning sound from panel

Dental

  • Severe pain / unbearable pain / worst pain / throbbing pain
  • Tooth knocked out / tooth fell out / lost a tooth
  • Broken jaw / swelling / can't open mouth / face swollen
  • Abscess / infection spreading / gum bleeding won't stop

Veterinary

  • Not breathing / stopped breathing / can't breathe
  • Ate something / swallowed something / possible poisoning / ingested
  • Seizure / having a seizure / collapsed
  • Hit by car / trauma / bleeding heavily / major injury
  • Bloated / swollen belly / distended stomach (critical for large dogs)

Legal

  • Arrested / being arrested / in custody / just arrested
  • Served / just served papers / received papers / served with lawsuit
  • Court tomorrow / hearing tomorrow / in court now
  • Restraining order / threatened / immediate danger

Notice what's not on any of these lists: the word "emergency" itself. When someone's pipe burst, they say "there's water everywhere." When someone's dog is seizing, they say "my dog is having a seizure." Train your AI on the words callers actually use, not the category labels you use internally.

Step 2: Build Your Tier 2 Urgent Keyword List

Tier 2 — the urgent-but-not-emergency tier — is what prevents your Tier 1 list from becoming overcrowded. Configure it separately with a different response: the AI takes detailed information, sends you a flagged SMS marked "URGENT," and tells the caller you'll personally call back within a specific window you choose (15 minutes, 30 minutes, or within the hour).

Tier 2 keywords describe problems that are time-sensitive without the physical urgency of Tier 1. Examples by industry:

HVAC & Plumbing (Tier 2)

  • Not working well / barely any heat / water barely hot / pressure low
  • Strange noise / banging / knocking / squealing
  • Commercial property / business is affected / shop is closed because of this
  • Elderly parent / elderly person in the house

Dental (Tier 2)

  • Crown fell off / veneer fell off / filling fell out
  • Broken tooth / chipped tooth / cracked tooth
  • Sensitivity / pain when eating / hurts when I drink something cold

Legal (Tier 2)

  • Deadline / filing deadline / responding to something
  • Settlement offer / they made an offer / need to respond
  • Employer terminating / about to lose job / HR meeting

The distinction between Tier 1 and Tier 2 comes down to one question: Is the damage actively getting worse while we talk? Burst pipe: yes, Tier 1. Low water pressure: no, Tier 2. Tooth knocked out: yes, Tier 1. Crown fell off: no, Tier 2.

Step 3: Understand What Happens During the Transfer (Don't Configure This Blind)

When CallBird detects a Tier 1 keyword, a specific sequence happens that most business owners don't know to account for in their configuration. Understanding the sequence lets you prepare the caller appropriately and avoid the two most common complaints: "The AI just put me on hold" and "I got transferred but no one picked up."

Here's the actual sequence:

  1. Keyword detected: The caller says a Tier 1 phrase. The AI recognizes it in real time, mid-sentence.
  2. Address and details captured: Before transferring, the AI asks for the caller's address, callback number, and a quick description of the situation — "So I can give the technician everything they need." This takes 30-45 seconds and gives you information you can act on immediately.
  3. SMS sent to you simultaneously: While the AI is still talking to the caller, you receive an SMS with the caller's name, number, address, and what they described. If you're unavailable to take the transfer, you have everything you need to call them back within two minutes.
  4. Transfer initiated: The AI tells the caller it's connecting them now and initiates the call transfer to your cell phone.
  5. If you don't answer: This is where most businesses leave a gap. Configure a fallback behavior — either a secondary number (a partner, on-call tech, or spouse), or a specific message the AI delivers if the transfer fails: "We weren't able to reach the technician directly. I've sent them your information as an emergency — they'll call you back within [X] minutes. Is there anything else you need while you wait?"

Step 5 is what separates good emergency routing from great emergency routing. The worst outcome isn't missing the transfer — it's missing the transfer AND leaving the caller with no expectation of what happens next. They'll call a competitor within 60 seconds.

Step 4: Calibrate to Avoid Alert Fatigue

Alert fatigue is the death of emergency routing. It happens when the Tier 1 list is too broad — "any mention of pain, any mention of damage" — and the business owner starts receiving 8-10 emergency transfers per day for calls that turn out to be routine. Within two to three weeks, they stop answering the transfers. Then the real emergencies start getting missed.

The calibration process is straightforward. After your first week live:

  1. Pull your call transcripts from the CallBird dashboard. Every call, every conversation.
  2. Find every call that triggered a Tier 1 transfer. Read what the caller actually said.
  3. For each one: Was this genuinely time-sensitive? Did it require immediate intervention? Or could it have been a Tier 2 priority SMS instead?
  4. Move anything that shouldn't be Tier 1 down to Tier 2. Add more specific language to the Tier 1 keywords — "no heat at all" instead of "heat problem."
  5. Repeat after week two. By week three, the routing should feel calibrated.

Most businesses land at a ratio of roughly one Tier 1 transfer per 15-25 calls. If you're getting more than that, your Tier 1 list is too broad. If you're getting fewer than one per 50 calls during a busy period, check whether the keywords match how your specific callers actually talk — regional language and terminology vary significantly.

The math on calibration: If your average emergency service call is worth $400-$800 and you're correctly routing even two additional emergencies per month, that's $800-$1,600 in captured revenue against a $99/month platform cost. The ROI is immediate — captured from day one of going live.

Industry-Specific Routing Configurations That Actually Work

Emergency routing isn't one-size-fits-all. The configuration that's right for an HVAC company is wrong for a dental practice. Here's what each industry needs that generic setups miss:

HVAC and Plumbing: Your emergency routing needs to include a service area verification step before the transfer. An emergency call from outside your service radius wastes everyone's time. Configure the AI to ask for zip code during the Tier 1 capture sequence. If it's outside your area, the AI stays on the line, declines gracefully, and can refer them to a general emergency hotline. Inside your area: full transfer sequence. For more on the full home services configuration, see the contractor AI receptionist guide.

Dental Practices: The complication in dental emergency routing is that some situations require your clinical judgment before you decide whether to come in. A knocked-out tooth: come in. Sensitivity to cold: Tier 2, schedule first thing tomorrow. Configure your AI to ask one qualifying question during Tier 1 capture — "Is this something you'd describe as severe, or more like significant discomfort?" — to help you triage before the transfer reaches you.

Veterinary Clinics: Many veterinary practices have an emergency clinic relationship for after-hours cases you can't handle. Configure a specific branch in your emergency flow: "For after-hours emergencies, we work with [Emergency Animal Hospital name] located at [address]. Would you like their number?" This serves the caller without requiring you to be on-call 24/7, while still answering the phone and providing value. See the veterinary AI receptionist guide for the full after-hours protocol.

Legal Practices: Legal emergencies almost never benefit from a 2am call to the attorney — they benefit from a detailed intake so the attorney can review before calling back at 8am. For legal, Tier 1 typically means "send the attorney an urgent SMS now AND a detailed transcript immediately," not necessarily a live transfer at midnight. The exception: criminal arrest situations, where immediate contact can materially affect outcomes.
