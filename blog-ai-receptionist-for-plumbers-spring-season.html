Why Is Spring the Hardest Season to Answer Your Phone?

Spring creates three simultaneous call surges that no other season matches — and that's exactly why it overwhelms your phone capacity. January is brutal for frozen pipes, but it's one problem type. August brings AC-adjacent plumbing issues, but it's moderate volume. Spring is different: thaw damage, sump pump emergencies, and renovation season all land in the same 10-week window, March through May.

Here's what happens to a typical plumbing company's call volume in spring:

  • Thaw damage calls — Pipes that froze and cracked in January don't always reveal themselves until late February or March, when water pressure returns through a damaged section. These are urgent, high-ticket jobs ($400–$1,200 depending on pipe access and repair scope).
  • Sump pump failures — Spring rains test sump pumps that sat dormant all winter. Homeowners discover failed floats and burned motors when their basement is already taking on water. Emergency calls, $300–$700 jobs, zero patience for voicemail.
  • Outdoor spigot damage — Freeze-thaw cycles crack outdoor hose bibs. Lower ticket ($150–$350) but high volume as temperatures swing.
  • Renovation season — Contractors who paused over winter restart bathroom remodels, kitchen updates, and water heater replacements. These are scheduled jobs, but they're calling five plumbers at once to see who responds first.

You're fielding all four categories simultaneously, while running the field work from those same four categories simultaneously. That's the spring phone problem. It's not one spike — it's four.
